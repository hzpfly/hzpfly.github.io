# What is CFD?
In the financial world, CFD stands for Contract for Difference.
It is a popular type of derivative trading that allows you to speculate on the price movements of fast-moving global financial markets—such as forex, stocks, indices, and commodities—without actually owning the underlying asset.

How a CFD Works

When you trade a CFD, you enter into a contract with a broker. You agree to exchange the difference in the price of an asset from the time you open the position to the time you close it.
• Going Long (Buying): If you think the price of an asset (like Gold or a currency pair) will go up, you buy a CFD. If the price rises, you make a profit. If it falls, you take a loss.
• Going Short (Selling): If you think the price will go down, you sell a CFD. You profit if the price drops, and you lose money if the price rises.

Key Characteristics of CFDs

• Leverage (Margin Trading): You only need to put down a small percentage of the total trade value (a deposit called "margin") to open a large position. While this amplifies potential profits, it also amplifies potential losses.
• No Asset Ownership: When you trade a EUR/USD CFD or an Apple stock CFD, you do not own any actual Euros or shares of Apple. You only hold a contract based on their price movements.
• Bi-directional Trading: You can potentially make money in both rising and falling markets.
